Tally is a privacy compliance and selective-disclosure service for Stellar confidential tokens. Its first use case is confidential one-to-many payouts: one funder pays many recipients, each amount stays private, and the round's total is provable to a donor on demand. It builds on Stellar's Confidential Tokens developer preview (OpenZeppelin v0.9.0, testnet).

The results are measured, not projected (re-measured 5 October 2026, protocol 29). A single zero-knowledge proof covering an entire round is 16,224 bytes, constant whether it covers 8 payments or 64; more recipients cost proving time, not proof size. A confidential transfer costs 91.2 million instructions, 22.8% of the per-transaction cap, and four transfers from one sender fit in one transaction, which we demonstrated on testnet. Our August figures under the earlier 100-million cap — one transfer per transaction, and on-chain aggregate verification at 100% of the budget — are superseded. Donor verification stays off-chain for privacy: putting it on chain would publish who received a disclosure and when.

One command runs a full round on testnet: 16 recipients across 5 lanes, no amount visible on any explorer, one proof, donor-verified. A published round can be checked by anyone with the repository and two values.

Built: the aggregate disclosure circuits, the round registry contract, a client SDK ported to OpenZeppelin v0.9.0 and checked against its published vectors, a standalone verification CLI, headless non-custodial registration, and the site. Not built, and marked so: any integration or user (we claim no adoption), a round with real independent contributors, durable verification beyond the RPC's seven-day event window, and every function of the compliance operator, which is a design.

A demo auditor key was committed in August; it was demo-only, is retired, and the current deployment's key has never been in the repository.
